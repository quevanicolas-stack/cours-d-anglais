The 5 Steps of a Sales Conversation in English

Every successful sale follows the same five steps

1
Prospecting / First Contact
Reaching out with a clear, immediate value hook.
2
Discovery
Understanding the real need before proposing anything.
3
Proposal
Presenting your offer, directly tied to what they told you.
4
Handling Objections
Addressing hesitation without pressure.
5
Closing
Confirming the deal and the next steps, clearly.

Leçon 2
Prospecting in English

How you open sets the tone for everything that follows

1
Punchy subject line
“Quick question about [their business/challenge]”
2
Personalized opening
“I came across your work on [platform] and was impressed by [specific thing].”
3
Immediate value
“I help [target] achieve [result] — and I think there could be a real fit here.”
4
Simple CTA
“Would you be open to a 15-minute call this week?”
Also works on LinkedIn“Hi [Name], I noticed you're working on [challenge] — I specialise in helping [target] with exactly that. Are you open to connecting?”

Language Focus
Stop Translating Your French Email Reflexes

A few politeness reflexes that read too formal in English

Reaching out
✕“I allow myself to contact you…”
✓“I wanted to reach out because…”
Following up
✕“Following our exchange…”
✓“Following up on our conversation…”
Staying available
✕“Don't hesitate to contact me.”
✓“Feel free to reach out” / “Let me know if you have any questions.”
Signing off
✕“I remain at your disposal.”
✓Just end with a next step or a warm closing line — no direct equivalent needed.

Leçon 3
Discovery Questions

The best questions feel like a conversation, not an interrogation

“What's the biggest challenge you're facing with [topic] right now?”

“What have you already tried?”

“What would success look like for you, six months from now?”

“What's driving the timing on this — why now?”

“On a scale of 1 to 10, how urgent is solving this for you?”

Leçon 4
Presenting Your Proposal with Impact

The proposal should feel like the obvious next step — not a pitch

1
Connect to the need
“Based on what you've shared, I think the best fit for you would be…”
2
Highlight value, not features
“This means you'll be able to…” — not “This includes…”
3
Use social proof
“One of my clients in a similar situation saw [result] within [timeframe].”
4
State the price with confidence
“The investment for this is [price].” — never apologize for the price.
